# Kazing!

## Who
Classroom instructors who run live quiz competitions from a laptop while students participate on phones, tablets, or computers.

## The struggle
An instructor has to prepare questions, distribute them through paper or a basic form, wait for responses, check answers, calculate scores, and announce results. This breaks the pace of the class, delays feedback, and makes it difficult to run a fair, energetic competition.

## Today's workaround
Instructors use paper quizzes, basic forms, or spreadsheets. These require manual marking or score calculation and do not give the class a synchronized question deadline, instant results, or a shared leaderboard.

## The MVP
Kazing! lets an instructor host a prepared multiple-choice quiz that students join with a six-digit Game PIN and play together from their browsers.

## How we'll know it worked
In a three-minute live demo, one instructor hosts a short quiz for several student devices. Students join with a PIN, answer before a shared deadline, and everyone sees the final leaderboard. A classroom instructor can try the flow before Session 3.

## Out of scope
- QR-code joining.
- Reconnecting a student after they lose their device or connection.
- Detailed per-question feedback.
- Advanced speed-based scoring.
- Full quiz authoring and editing in the initial demo slice.
- Long-term quiz history and analytics.

## Glossary
- **Instructor**: the person hosting and controlling a live quiz.
- **Student**: a participant who joins a live quiz with a Game PIN and display name.
- **Game PIN**: a unique six-digit code students use to join a session.
- **Session**: one live run of a prepared quiz.
- **Question deadline**: the server-stored UTC time after which answers are rejected.
- **Leaderboard**: the ranked list of student scores shown during or after a session.
