# Kazing! pitch

## Script

**Speaker 1:** Picture a classroom quiz run by an instructor with thirty students waiting for the next question. The instructor has questions ready, but running the activity means handing out paper or opening a basic form, waiting for answers, checking them, adding scores, and trying to keep the room interested. By the time the scores are ready, the energy has gone.

**Speaker 2:** Today, instructors work around this with paper, forms, or spreadsheets. Those tools collect answers, but they do not run a live game. They do not give every student the same deadline, stop duplicate answers, or show a shared ranking without more manual work.

**Speaker 1:** Kazing! is a browser-based classroom quiz game. An instructor hosts a prepared multiple-choice quiz from a laptop. Students open Kazing! on their phones, enter a six-digit Game PIN and a display name, then join the same live session. The instructor starts each question, students answer before the shared deadline, and Kazing! calculates the score and displays the ranking.

**Speaker 2:** In our Session 3 demo, we will show an instructor start a short quiz with several student devices. You will see students join through the PIN, receive the same question, submit one answer before the timer ends, and reach a final leaderboard together.

**Speaker 1:** We will know it works when a classroom instructor can run that flow without paper marking or manual score calculation, and when students can participate from the browsers they already have.

## Likely questions

### Why not use Google Forms?
Forms can collect answers, but the MVP needs a host-controlled live session, a shared deadline, one accepted answer per student, and an immediate class leaderboard. These are the game loop Kazing! is designed to run.

### Have you observed this with a specific instructor?
The product specification clearly identifies the workflow, but it does not record a named instructor or a direct observation. Before the presentation, the team should validate the scenario with one instructor and add that concrete account to the pitch.

### Is this too large for the workshop?
The full Kazing roadmap is large. The demo scope is intentionally limited to one prepared quiz, PIN joining, one host-controlled question flow, answer collection, and a final leaderboard. Quiz editing, QR codes, reconnect recovery, and advanced scoring are parked for later.
